Evidence appendix

All results come from one discrete-event simulator that implements the brief's network exactly (moving-receiver light time, solar exclusion on every launch, 19 links, FIFO serialization, relay processing, hop and endpoint timers, handshakes, quotas, maintenance, incidents, per-launch loss) and our v3 ledgers. Every traced run checks conservation, non-negativity, hold arithmetic and registry custody after every ledger event. A trace is a conditional, no-loss run unless labelled "lossy". Times are hours after the scenario start; the epoch is 2026-09-22 00:00 TDB. "Bound" and "exact" are stated wherever a probability appears.

E1. Orbital calculations

Method. Each body follows the fixed Kepler ellipse in data.zip: mean anomaly M = M₀ + n·t (n from the supplied mean motion), Kepler's equation solved by Newton iteration until the residual is below 10⁻¹⁵ rad, then rotation by ω, i, Ω into the Sun-centred J2000 ecliptic frame. Units: AU and seconds of TDB since the epoch (hours in tables). The relays use the same code with e = 0 and the supplied phases. Light time: a packet emitted at t_e from P reaches Q at the t_a solving t_a = t_e + 499.02·|Q(t_a) − P(t_e)| (8.317 min/AU), found by fixed-point iteration; for Earth→A the successive corrections are 2.1e-02, 4.1e-07, 7.7e-12 s (each step shrinks by v/c ≈ 10⁻⁴), so two iterations reach 1 ms and we always iterate to 10⁻⁶ s. Solar exclusion: distance from the Sun to the segment P(t_e)→Q(t_a), blocked below 0.10 AU. Every launch, ACK and receipt is evaluated at its own emission time. Maximum epoch error against the brief's table: 7.3e-07 AU (table rounding).

S2. Stress and recovery

How we chose the incident. We ran the futures scenario (both price paths) under every allowed incident on a grid: 72 h isolation of each settlement starting every hour, 6 h forced loss at each of the 11 nodes every 0.5 h, and an endpoint reset of each exchange every 0.25 h, all starting between h 0 and 420: 56,280 runs. Chosen: endpoint reset of the Mars exchange at h 0.75, the worst in both paths. It is not the obvious "isolate the host at payout time" (which costs about 75 h) but a short, silent event. At h 0.75 Mars has just answered Alice's and Cara's handshakes; the reset wipes its session state, but its SYN-ACKs are already in flight. Earth and Ceres therefore believe the sessions are up and send their funding transfers into sessions Mars no longer knows: Mars ignores them, as the brief requires. The senders learn nothing until four endpoint attempts of R_e ≈ 25 h have expired, then wait the 24 h application backoff, then resubmit. E4. Long-horizon scan — claiming Tier 3

Scan. 200 Julian years from the epoch at a 1 h step (1,753,201 epochs) for all 38 directed backbone links and all 72 directed direct paths, moving-receiver clearance at each sample, maintenance not repeated. Runtime 43 min on 20 cores. Shortest closure we could miss: clearance is the Sun's distance to the segment sender(t_e)→receiver(t_a), which changes no faster than the fastest endpoint plus the arrival shift: L ≤ 0.00146 AU/h (Mercury at perihelion, 58.98 km/s). A closure entirely between two samples would leave a sample below 0.10 + L = 0.10146 AU. Every sample below that guard is re-examined on a 10 s grid and every open/closed change is bisected to 1 ms, so no closure of any length is missed. Routes: best-route delay from every settlement to each market host and back, at 6 h steps, sequential flights.

Refined boundary (Tier 2). Earth → Relay A, first closure: hourly bracket [24001200, 24004800] s, bisected on the exact light-time solver to [24004585.374355, 24004585.375214] s (width 0.858 ms, tolerance 1 ms). A packet emitted before the boundary flies on its own path and is unaffected; one emitted after it is never launched by our queue (the link waits), so no packet in flight is lost by a closure: the closure only delays the next launch, and the hop timer R_h is restarted at the actual emission.

Backbone: Relay A–B never closes (min clearance 2.00 AU). Gateway links: availability · number of closures in 200 y · longest closure. Routes: one-way minutes min–median–max over 200 y. Direct to Mars: availability · longest closure.
